Research becomes infrastructure.

Serious quantitative research takes more than a good model. It needs reliable data, infrastructure for experiments, realistic simulation, risk controls, monitoring, and systems that others can reproduce.

This page describes our approach to research infrastructure. Specific systems and tools will be documented here as they are published. [PLACEHOLDER — link real components as they become public]

Lifecycle

From hypothesis to evaluation — and back.

  1. 01

    Research

    Form a falsifiable hypothesis and state what would disprove it.

    What is the claim?
  2. 02

    Models

    Formalise the idea with explicit, documented assumptions.

    What is assumed?
  3. 03

    Data

    Versioned, point-in-time inputs with known provenance.

    Can it be trusted?
  4. 04

    Simulation

    Test against realistic costs, constraints, and regimes.

    Does it survive friction?
  5. 05

    Execution

    Implement carefully, with risk controls and monitoring.

    Is it safe to run?
  6. 06

    Evaluation

    Compare outcomes with expectations and record the difference.

    What did we learn?

Evaluation feeds back into research. The loop is the system.

Requirements

What reliable research systems need.

These are the properties we design for. They are easy to state and hard to achieve, which is why they are worth building in the open.

Data

Point-in-time correctness

Inputs reflect only what was knowable at each moment, avoiding look-ahead and survivorship bias.

Experiments

Reproducible environments

Pinned dependencies, versioned data, and recorded configurations, so a result can be rebuilt exactly.

Simulation

Realistic frictions

Transaction costs, slippage, capacity, and constraints are modelled explicitly, not added as an afterthought.

Risk

Controls by construction

Limits and safeguards are part of the system design, tested like any other component.

Operations

Monitoring

Behaviour in production is observed and compared against what research predicted.

Governance

Auditability

Every decision, from data selection to parameter choice, leaves a record that others can follow.
